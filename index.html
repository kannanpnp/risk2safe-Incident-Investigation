Step 1 — Plan the Investigation

Record the basic facts. Then start your Spring SnapCharT® (Step 2) to develop an initial picture of what happened, decide what evidence to collect, and list witnesses to interview.

Evidence & interview planning

Planning prompts from the methodology: What fragile evidence needs immediate preservation? Who needs to be interviewed and in what order? What procedures and policies were (or should have been) used? What facts need two independent sources to verify? What are the legal/regulatory implications?
checks completeness of facts, evidence & interview plan

Step 2 — Build the SnapCharT® (sequence of Events)

Ask “What happened next?” — add Events (rectangles, “who did what”, active verbs, one action each) left → right in real time, before and after the Incident (circle — the worst event, the reason you are investigating). Attach Conditions (ovals, passive “was/were” statements — the rest of the story) under the Event they explain. Use dashed borders for unproven information, assumptions, and open questions; switch to solid when proven.

Select an Event card, then:
Event — action, “who did what” Incident — worst event Condition — “rest of the story” Dashed — unproven / assumption / question ▲ Causal Factor
Quality checks (Summer → Autumn SnapCharT®): Is every Event factual, non-judgmental, and quantified where possible? Are date/times shown? Have you collected evidence to turn dashed shapes solid? Does the story flow without gaps? Conditions about procedures, training, quality checks, communications, policies, human engineering and work direction will drive the root cause analysis — capture them now.
flags gaps in the timeline, wording issues, missing Conditions & unproven info

Step 3 — Define Causal Factors

A Causal Factor is a mistake, error, or failure that, if corrected, could have prevented the Incident or significantly mitigated its consequences — typically a human error or an equipment malfunction. Most incidents have multiple Causal Factors.

The 4-Step Method: ① Identify all problems on the SnapCharT® (big and small — use the “problem?” toggle on each shape). ② Group related Conditions near the Event they impact. ③ Use the “So what?” method — start with each problem and ask “so what resulted from that?” until you reach an Event or the Incident. ④ The Causal Factor is that first Event or one–two “so whats” back: Who did what wrong? or What equipment failed or didn’t work as intended? Mark it with the ▲ triangle on the chart.

Safeguard / Barrier analysis

List the barriers/safeguards that stood between the hazard and the people/assets. Status: Failed (existed but did not work), Not present (should have existed), Success (worked and limited the consequences). Each failed or missing barrier usually points to a Causal Factor — and feeds section 4 of the report.

checks the 4-Step Method: are these the right big-picture CFs? Any missed?

Step 4 — Analyze each Causal Factor with the Root Cause Tree®

Analyze each Causal Factor one at a time. Start at the top of the tree, answer the dictionary questions honestly (“yes” = the category applies), and drill down to specific root causes. A Causal Factor may have one, several, or — rarely — no root causes.

validates 15-Question answers & selected root causes against the chart evidence

Step 4b — Human Factors analysis (DoD HFACS 8.0)

For each human-error Causal Factor, classify the contributing human factors across the four HFACS tiers: Unsafe Acts (active failures — the last actions/inactions before the mishap) → Preconditions → Supervisory/Leadership influences → Organizational influences (latent failures that lay dormant in the system). HFACS expects at least one Precondition for every Unsafe Act. Selections feed the “Active and Latent failures” section of the report.

checks tier coverage & suggests applicable nanocodes from the evidence

Step 5 — Develop & track Corrective Actions

Develop a fix for every root cause. Test each action against SMARTER: Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Reasonable, Timely, Effective, Reviewed. Prefer fixes that remove the hazard or improve the system over “re-train and remind”.

SMARTER test for each action + stronger fixes up the hierarchy of controls
